# Chapter 1 — Hong Kong, 2027 (Sham Shui Po)

Reference document for chapter-1 content (PLAN §7.6, §12.2). This file is the single point
where the chapter's picture of the real street is reviewed by a human; the NPC `lore` fields
are digests of it. The fiction sits on top of it: **every character is invented, the
geography is real.** NPCs never claim to be, or to know, a real living person, and never
state facts about named real businesses, institutions or officials beyond what is recorded
here.

## The street as it is (daily-life texture, not politics)

- Sham Shui Po MTR station feeds the district from exits A/B/C/D; the D exit side is the
  market end, where the pavement belongs to stalls first and pedestrians second.
- Pei Ho Street (北河街) runs the wet market: tarpaulins, crates of vegetables, fish tanks
  on the pavement, shutter-halves rolled up at dawn and down by evening.
- Apliu Street (鴨寮街) is the flea market: tables of cables, old phones, tools, radios —
  a street where everything has a second life and a price that moves.
- Tong lau (tenement blocks): six to eight storeys, ground-floor shops, stairwells with
  brass mailboxes and no lifts; many blocks wear scaffolding netting or have a notice about
  redevelopment stuck to the gate.
- Cha chaan teng: booth seats against the wall, milk tea in thick cups, a menu board with
  chalk prices, two speeds of staff — one fast, one who talks.
- The district holds both eras at once: cashless payment on one counter, a hand-written
  price sticker on the next; delivery riders threading the same lane as a man carrying
  half a wardrobe on a trolley.

## The fictional layer

- The player's family runs a small sundry shop on Pei Ho Street that is closing down; the
  family is moving out, and the flat above the shop is being emptied box by box.
- The shop's wooden sign has come down; the street remembers the family better than the
  ledger does.
- Five neighbours matter: the cha chaan teng owner who knew the family, the retiree with
  the bird cage who is the street's memory, a delivery rider who sees everything that
  changed, an architecture student documenting the tong lau, and the building's caretaker.

## Rules for this chapter (from §7.6 — "real streets, fictional people")

- Every conflict is personal, not institutional: a family deciding what to keep, an owner
  retiring, a letter that was never sent.
- Geography may be named; businesses and institutions may only be described generically.
- NPCs carry explicit `taboos` in their persona, and deflect in character (a persona
  gambit, not a refusal) if the player steers toward current affairs.
- Modern NPCs speak of 2027 as daily life — work, family, food, money, neighbours, the
  street — and of the past only what the mementos and their memories carry.
