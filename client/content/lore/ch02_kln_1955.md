# Chapter 2 — Kowloon, 1955

Reference document for chapter-2 content (PLAN §7.6, §12.1). This file is the single point
where the chapter's picture of 1955 Kowloon is reviewed by a human; the NPC `lore` fields are
digests of it. Scope follows §7.6 for historical chapters: **daily life, work, migration,
food, family, language** — lived experience, not contemporary political dispute, and only
what this file asserts.

## The street as it was (daily-life texture)

- The Kowloon market ran on hawker licences and hand signals: baskets on the pavement at
  dawn, licenced stalls by day, everything packed away before the lanterns went up.
- Squatter huts climbed the hillsides behind the market: timber frames, tarpaulin and
  corrugated iron roofs, kerosene lamps, water carried up in buckets from the standpipe.
- Rationing-era staples: rice bought by the catty with tickets, charcoal by the basket,
  condensed milk saved for the children. Tea was the cheap comfort — a stall could live on
  it.
- A shop's first stock was bought one crate at a time and priced by hand. Opening day
  meant firecrackers on the step if you could afford them, a free pot of tea if you
  couldn't.
- **Fires were the terror of the hut settlements.** Timber and tarpaulin burn fast, and
  the settlements burned more than once in these years; a whole hillside could go in an
  afternoon. What a family rebuilt, they rebuilt smaller. (This is the general, true
  record of the era; the specific fire and the people it touched in this chapter are
  fiction, as everything below is.)

## The fictional layer

- The tea stall by the market belongs to a young woman the street calls 阿彩 — she is the
  player's grandmother, twenty-something in 1955, and her milk tea is already famous:
  no sugar, extra hot, for the hawkers before dawn.
- 華叔 — in 1955 a hawker in his thirties — is the one who opens the family shop that
  year: first stock one crate at a time, prices by hand, a pot of tea kept going all day
  for anyone carrying a load. (The player met him old in chapter 1; here he is young and
  does not know the player, and must not be told who they are.)
- 阿好 is a hut-settlement neighbour: her cooked-food stall burned in a settlement fire,
  and she now sells from a basket she carries on her arm. What she lost is personal, and
  she tells it only to someone patient.
- The album the player carries is from this street: these are the faces in its black
  pages, taken the year the shop opened.

## Rules for this chapter (from §7.6)

- Only assert what this file records; dates, names and prices beyond it are deflectable
  in character ("我邊記得咁細緻，自己翻下本相簿啦").
- The player is a stranger with an impossible object (the album). NPCs react to the
  object, never accept a claim about the future, and never suspect time travel — a
  coincidence of faces is the only explanation anyone offers.
- Conflicts stay personal: a stall to rebuild, a debt to a neighbour, a wedding dress
  kept for someone who did not come back. No institutional or political storylines.
